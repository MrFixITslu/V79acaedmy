# From Idea to Advantage — Curriculum v2 Learning Standard

## Graduate outcome

A learner who completes **From Idea to Advantage** should be able to make and implement better small-business decisions using:

**DEFINE → MODEL → TEST → CONTROL → MEASURE → IMPROVE**

The course is designed for Caribbean entrepreneurs and operators. Completion should mean the learner can apply the framework to a new business situation—not only fill in a workbook about their current business.

## Every lesson must provide measurable value

### Lesson 1 — Understand the decision
Each module's first lesson must include:
- the management decision;
- an operator mental model;
- core principles;
- a worked Caribbean example;
- a realistic business scenario;
- red-team questions;
- a decision note separating evidence, belief and uncertainty;
- a teach-back check.

### Lesson 2 — Build the system
Each module's second lesson must include:
- operator moves;
- an implementation method;
- practical actions;
- decision tools where relevant;
- an implementation drill;
- failure-proofing questions;
- a handoff test so the system is not dependent on the owner explaining everything verbally.

### Lesson 3 — Control and prove
Each module's third lesson must include:
- a control loop;
- decision-relevant measures;
- common failure warning;
- red-team review;
- evidence of mastery;
- transfer to a new scenario;
- a 30-day proof requirement;
- a management evidence pack showing baseline → decision → action → measure → result → next decision.

## Business Operator habits

Graduates should repeatedly demonstrate:

1. **DEFINE** the real constraint or management decision.
2. **MODEL** the customer, cash, capacity, cost and risk assumptions.
3. **TEST** the weakest assumption using real evidence or a low-cost experiment.
4. **CONTROL** execution with owner, rule, limit, checklist and escalation.
5. **MEASURE** the small set of indicators that change a decision.
6. **IMPROVE** by continuing, changing, stopping or sequencing the next action.

## Module 12 — Business Operator Benchmark

The final benchmark should use a **new scenario**, not simply the learner's own business plan.

The scenario should include:
- a growth opportunity;
- at least one cash, customer, supplier, people, operational, technology or resilience shock;
- incomplete information that forces the learner to distinguish fact from assumption.

The learner must:
- diagnose the constraint;
- show important calculations/assumptions;
- identify the weakest assumption;
- propose evidence or a test;
- define controls, owners and limits;
- choose decision-relevant KPIs;
- create a 90-day response;
- define continue/change/stop rules;
- state the next review date.

## Graduation rubric

Score 1–4 across:

1. **DEFINE + PRIORITIZE**
2. **MODEL + NUMBERS**
3. **TEST + EVIDENCE**
4. **CONTROL + OWNERSHIP**
5. **MEASURE + DECIDE**
6. **TRANSFER + EXPLAIN**

Recommended pass standard: **no category below 3 — Competent**.

## Red-team failure conditions

The course is weakened if future edits:
- reduce lessons to business advice with no decision process;
- reward polished plans without evidence;
- use revenue without margin/cash/capacity context;
- treat compliments or social engagement as demand proof;
- treat AI adoption as automatically beneficial;
- omit owners, limits or escalation from systems;
- use KPIs that do not change a decision;
- remove Caribbean operating realities;
- remove transfer to a new scenario;
- allow the final plan to pass without assumptions, stop rules or review dates.

## Value test

For every lesson ask:

> **What decision can the learner make, system can they build, or control can they operate more reliably after this lesson?**

If the answer is unclear, the lesson needs stronger teaching, practice or assessment.
