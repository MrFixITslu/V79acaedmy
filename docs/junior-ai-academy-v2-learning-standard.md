# V79 Junior AI Academy v2 — AI Efficiency Learning Standard

> **Superseded:** retained for historical reference. The active course standard is `docs/junior-ai-academy-v3-learning-standard.md` (curriculum v3.1).

## Why v2 exists

The original course had a strong project structure, safety model, media plan and teamwork system, but too many learner lessons stopped at “know the idea” or “make an artifact.”

The v2 standard raises the graduation goal:

> **A learner should be able to use AI efficiently, deliberately and responsibly—not just ask AI for things.**

Efficiency means the learner can:

1. **Define the task** before prompting.
2. **Choose whether AI is the right tool** for that part of the task.
3. **Give useful context** without oversharing.
4. **Set constraints and output format** so results are easier to use and check.
5. **Use focused follow-ups** instead of restarting blindly.
6. **Verify important claims** with external evidence.
7. **Compare options against human-chosen criteria.**
8. **Break complex work into steps** with clean handoffs and quality gates.
9. **Reuse prompts, checklists and workflows** instead of rebuilding from scratch.
10. **Explain what AI did, what the human decided, and what was checked.**

## V79 AI Efficiency Loop

Every mission now reinforces:

**DEFINE → CHOOSE → PROMPT → CHECK → IMPROVE → SAVE**

- **DEFINE** — What result do I actually need?
- **CHOOSE** — Is AI the best tool for this part?
- **PROMPT** — Give context, constraints and a useful output format.
- **CHECK** — Inspect instructions, facts, safety and usefulness.
- **IMPROVE** — Fix the weak part with a focused follow-up.
- **SAVE** — Keep reusable prompts, examples, checklists and workflows.

## Lesson-value standard

Each of the 48 lessons must deliver more than a topic introduction.

### Discover lessons
Every Discover lesson includes:

- mission-specific efficiency skill;
- key concepts and vocabulary;
- when AI is useful;
- when AI is not the right choice;
- a weak-vs-strong worked example;
- age-appropriate application;
- safety/ethics check;
- teach-back and reflection.

### Create lessons
Every Create lesson includes:

- step-by-step skill recipe;
- reusable prompt/workflow pattern;
- guided practice;
- common failure patterns and fixes;
- focused follow-up prompting;
- structured-output habit;
- transfer challenge;
- explicit quality checklist;
- human contribution evidence.

### Studio lessons
Every Studio lesson includes:

- team project application;
- AI-vs-human/simple-tool task choice;
- process evidence requirement;
- before/after improvement;
- quality criteria;
- risk management;
- reusable Team AI Playbook entry;
- individual exit ticket;
- instructor review/resubmission.

## Prompt patterns learners should graduate with

Learners should be able to adapt reusable patterns for:

- **Generate:** multiple options under clear constraints.
- **Summarize:** using only provided material.
- **Extract:** pull specific details into a list/table.
- **Transform:** rewrite for a new audience, tone or format while preserving facts.
- **Compare:** evaluate alternatives against stated criteria.
- **Explain:** explain at the learner’s level with examples.
- **Critique:** check a learner-created draft against a rubric without taking over authorship.
- **Research planning:** generate questions/keywords, then verify externally.
- **Quality audit:** inspect an artifact against acceptance criteria.
- **Workflow design:** specify inputs, owners, outputs, dependencies and checks.

## Graduate evidence

A learner should leave the course with:

- 5–10 reusable prompt templates;
- at least two reusable AI-assisted workflows;
- a personal AI-or-not decision rule;
- a fact-checking method;
- a privacy/data-minimization rule;
- a quality-audit checklist;
- before/after prompt and revision examples;
- a Team/Personal AI Playbook;
- final project evidence showing what AI did and what humans decided.

## Assessment upgrade

Each mission now has five quiz questions:

- three concept questions;
- two scenario/application questions.

Scenario questions test whether learners can apply the efficiency skill rather than merely recognize terminology.

## Migration standard

## Red-team success criteria

The v2 course should fail review if any mission:

- teaches only vocabulary without a usable method;
- asks for an artifact without teaching how to create/check it;
- treats AI as the default tool;
- omits human verification;
- rewards polished output without process evidence;
- cannot produce something reusable beyond that week;
- depends on “AI says so” as justification;
- or leaves the learner unable to explain what they would do differently on a new task.
